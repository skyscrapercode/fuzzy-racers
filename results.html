How Fuzzy Logic Works

What is it?

Fuzzy logic is a way of reasoning with degrees of truth rather than hard yes/no answers. A fact like "the player is close" can be 0.7 true instead of just true or false, so multiple soft conditions can blend together into a smooth decision.

Why use it for racing AI?

Race telemetry is continuous and messy — distance, speed, cornering, health, gap. A traditional if/else AI either explodes with edge cases or jerks between behaviors at hard thresholds. A fuzzy controller weighs every signal at once and produces a smooth, defensible action — exactly what you want from a believable opponent.

The 3-step pipeline

STEP 1
Fuzzification
Raw race telemetry (distance px, speed px/s, health HP…) is converted into degrees of membership in fuzzy sets like very_close, fast, critical.
▸
STEP 2
Rule Evaluation
33 rules of the form IF … THEN … fire in parallel. Each rule's strength is the min() of its conditions (Mamdani AND). Multiple rules can fire on the same output, and the activations are aggregated by max().
▸
STEP 3
Defuzzification
The aggregate output membership curve is collapsed to a single crisp number via the centroid method. That number becomes the AI's throttle / brake / steering command for this tick.

What in this game is fuzzy?

  • Speed control — throttle and brake are chosen by corner sharpness and distance to the player, not hard thresholds.
  • Powerup timing — when to fire a held powerup is computed from gap, health, and "powerup ready" simultaneously.
  • Aggression — the AI's overall posture (defensive vs. attacking) is a fuzzy blend of gap, health and proximity.
  • Braking under risk — at critical health the AI brakes earlier and softens aggression even on straight track.
